44. TRUST LAYER — THE CORE DIFFERENTIATOR

Every major competitor in this market (Tumbledry, UClean, Fabrico, The Laundry 
Basket, Neeravi, JOBOY, etc.) suffers from the same complaint pattern:

- Processing/charging clothes BEFORE customer approval
- No proof of item condition at pickup, so damage/loss disputes go nowhere
- Centralized "plant" model with no accountable local owner
- Customer support that stalls, denies, and bounces the complaint around

Washy Washy's entire product wedge is closing these gaps mechanically, not
just promising to. Build the following as HARD WORKFLOW RULES, not optional
UI decoration:

44.1 APPROVAL LOCK
The Laundry Partner Dashboard must NOT allow "Start Processing" to be pressed
until order.priceApprovalStatus == "approved" by the customer.
If a partner tries to start processing on an unapproved order, block the 
action and show: "Waiting on customer price approval."
Every approval must be timestamped and stored immutably against the order 
(approval log), visible to admin and customer in Order Details.

44.2 MANDATORY PHOTO EVIDENCE
Driver app: "Take photo" at pickup is NOT optional — cannot tap "Confirm 
Pickup" without at least 1 photo of the bag/items.
Same rule applies at delivery handoff.
Customer-facing Order Details screen shows a small "Condition at pickup" 
thumbnail the customer can tap to view full-size.
This photo becomes the default evidence attached to any complaint filed 
against that order — auto-attach it, don't make the customer hunt for it.

44.3 VISIBLE ACCOUNTABILITY CHAIN
On Order Details (customer-facing, simplified) show:
"Handled by [Local Partner Name] near you"
Do NOT show internal ops jargon, but DO show a real named local partner —
this builds trust that a faceless corporate plant can't.
Admin's chain-of-custody timeline (per section 29) must be exactly what 
auto-populates when a complaint is opened, so resolution time is minutes, 
not days.

44.4 COMPLAINT SLA VISIBILITY
Complaint screen (customer-facing) must show an expected response time,
e.g. "We typically respond within 4 hours."
Admin dashboard's complaint queue must show an SLA countdown per complaint, 
color-coded, and escalate/flag anything unresolved past SLA — this is 
Attention Required territory, same pattern as section 28.


45. "NO SURPRISE" PRICING UX (STRENGTHEN SECTION 16)

The price-change card (section 16) is not just a UI nicety — it is the 
product's #1 marketing claim in disguise. Reinforce it visually:

- Give the approval card its own moment: full-screen or prominent modal, 
  not a small banner, so it can't be missed or accidentally dismissed.
- Show a one-line reason in plain language ("2 items needed stain 
  treatment"), never a vague "price adjusted."
- Add a small trust microcopy line permanently visible during booking and 
  order tracking: "We never process or charge without your approval."
- This same line should appear in onboarding/first-launch screens as a 
  core brand promise, not buried in T&Cs.


46. APARTMENT COMMUNITY GROWTH LOOP (STRENGTHEN SECTION 21)

Treat apartment collection as a growth/marketing surface, not just an 
operational convenience:

- On the apartment collection card, add a light social-proof element:
  "12 residents scheduled — join this pickup" with a single-tap join CTA.
- Add a referral micro-flow specifically for apartment residents: 
  "Invite your building" — generates a shareable link/code tied to that 
  apartment's collection day, with a small credit reward for both sides.
- Admin dashboard needs an "Apartments" view: list of onboarded 
  communities, resident count, next collection day, fill rate — this is 
  how ops decides which buildings to prioritize for local marketing pushes 
  (flyers, resident WhatsApp groups, RWA tie-ups).


47. TRUST-FIRST ONBOARDING / FIRST-LAUNCH SCREENS

Add a short (2–3 screen, skippable) first-launch sequence before Home, 
each screen pairing one product mechanic with the trust problem it solves:

Screen 1: "Track every step" — bag QR + timeline preview
Screen 2: "No surprise charges" — price approval card preview
Screen 3: "Real local partners near you" — named partner + apartment 
collection preview

Keep it visual, minimal text, skip button always visible top-right.
This onboarding IS the marketing pitch — it should look like a premium 
consumer app's onboarding, not a tutorial.


48. LOADER

Use the Washy Washy icon as the loading indicator across all four apps 
(customer, driver, partner, admin) — consistent brand touchpoint even 
during wait states. No generic spinners.
